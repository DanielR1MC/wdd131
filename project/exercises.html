General Biomechanics Principles

Master the mechanics behind effective hypertrophy, optimal joint alignment, and smart exercise sequencing.

1. Movement Patterns (Push / Pull / Legs)

  • Push: Movements pushing load away from the body. Targets anterior chain (Chest, Front Delts, Triceps).
  • Pull: Movements pulling load toward the body. Targets posterior chain (Lats, Traps, Rhomboids, Rear Delts, Biceps).
  • Legs (Knee vs. Hip Dominant):
    • Knee-Dominant: High knee flexion focusing on Quads (e.g., Squats, Leg Press).
    • Hip-Dominant: Hip hinge movement focusing on Glutes & Hamstrings (e.g., RDLs, Hip Thrusts).

2. Muscle Roles & Joint Control

  • Agonist (Prime Mover): The primary muscle responsible for generating force.
  • Synergist (Assistant): Muscles supporting the movement and preventing unwanted rotation.
  • Stabilizer / Antagonist: Contracts isometrically to maintain posture (e.g., Core in Overhead Press) or stretches to allow smooth joint motion.

3. Phases of Muscle Contraction

  • Concentric (Positive Phase): Muscle shortens under tension to overcome resistance (e.g., pressing the bar up).
  • Eccentric (Negative Phase): Muscle lengthens under control. Crucial phase for micro-tears and hypertrophy stimulus.
  • Isometric: Force production without joint angle change (e.g., pause at bottom of a squat)

4. Compound vs. Isolation (Workout Order)

  • Compound (Multi-Joint): Involves 2+ joints and large muscle groups. High CNS demand. Place at the start of your workout when energy is peak
  • Isolation (Single-Joint): Focuses tension on a specific muscle. Place toward the middle/end to accumulate volume safely

5. Alignment & Line of Force

Muscle fibers recruit optimally when resistance direction aligns with fiber direction. Adjusting working angles (e.g., 30° incline bench for Upper Chest) maximizes target muscle engagement and reduces joint stress
